To convert a booking code, a tool reads the selections stored in the original code, finds the same matches on your chosen bookmaker, maps each market to its equivalent, and generates a new betslip or code you can load there. That means a SportyBet code shared by a tipster can become a Bet9ja, Betway or BetKing slip without you rebuilding it by hand. This guide explains how conversion works, why some selections cannot be carried across, and how to use the OddsBridge [booking code converter](/booking-code-converter) safely.

## Why you need to convert a booking code

Booking codes are locked to the bookmaker that created them. If you are new to them, start with our explainer on [what a booking code is](/guides/what-is-a-booking-code). The problem is that tipsters rarely share codes for every bookmaker. You might follow a Ghanaian tipster who posts [SportyBet](/bookmakers/sportybet) codes while your account is with [Betway](/bookmakers/betway), or a Nigerian group that only shares Bet9ja codes when you prefer BetKing.

Rebuilding a 10-leg slip manually is slow and it is easy to make mistakes: picking the wrong team in a match with similar names, choosing over 1.5 instead of over 2.5, or missing a selection entirely. Conversion automates that work and flags anything it cannot match.

## How booking code conversion works

Behind the scenes, converting a code involves four main steps.

### 1. Decoding the original slip

The converter asks the source bookmaker what the code contains. The result is a list of selections, each with an event (for example Arsenal v Chelsea), a market (1X2) and an outcome (Home). Some bookmakers also return kickoff times, league names and the odds at the time the slip was saved.

### 2. Matching the events

Different bookmakers name teams differently. One might list "Man Utd", another "Manchester United", and a third "Manchester Utd FC". A good converter normalises team names, handles common abbreviations and alternative spellings, and then confirms the match using the **kickoff time** and **competition**. Kickoff is especially important: two clubs can meet more than once in a season, and youth or women's teams often share names with the senior men's side.

### 3. Mapping the markets

Each bookmaker has its own internal names for markets. The converter translates them so that the meaning stays identical. The most common markets map cleanly:

| Market | What it means | Usually converts cleanly? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1X2 (match result) | Home win, draw or away win in 90 minutes | Yes |
| Double chance | Two of the three outcomes (1X, 12, X2) | Yes |
| Over/under goals | Total goals above or below a line, such as 2.5 | Yes, if the same line is offered |
| Both teams to score | Yes or no | Yes |
| Draw no bet | Stake refunded on a draw | Usually |
| Handicaps | Asian or European handicap lines | Only if the identical line exists |
| Player and corner markets | Goalscorers, cards, corners | Often not |

For a deeper look at these markets, see [betting markets explained](/guides/betting-markets-explained).

### 4. Building the new slip

Once every selection is matched, the converter creates an equivalent slip on the target bookmaker and gives you a new code or link to load it. It should also show you the odds on the new bookmaker for each selection so you can compare totals.

## Why some selections cannot convert

Even the best converter cannot carry every selection across. These are the usual reasons:

- **The market is not offered.** The target bookmaker may not price a specific market, such as "over 3.5 corners first half" or a particular handicap line.
- **The event is not listed.** Smaller leagues, friendlies and lower divisions are covered by some bookmakers and not others.
- **The match has already started.** Pre-match selections cannot be converted once kickoff passes.
- **A different line is offered.** If the source slip has over 2.25 goals and the target only offers 2.0 or 2.5, the converter should not guess.
- **The market is suspended.** Bookmakers temporarily suspend markets around team news or late price moves.
- **Rules differ.** Some markets settle differently between bookmakers, for instance whether extra time counts. Where the rules are not equivalent, a careful converter flags the selection rather than swapping in something similar.

When a selection cannot be matched, the converter should tell you clearly, so you can choose to drop it, replace it yourself or keep the slip on the original bookmaker.

## Verify before you place the bet

Conversion is a time saver, not a substitute for checking. Before you stake:

- **Confirm every selection.** Check team names, the market and the outcome. "Over 2.5" and "under 2.5" look similar on a small screen.
- **Check the total odds.** Odds differ between bookmakers, so the converted slip will rarely have exactly the same total as the original.
- **Count the legs.** If the original had 12 selections and the converted slip has 11, find out which one dropped and why.
- **Compare prices.** It might be worth placing the slip on a third bookmaker with better odds. Our [odds comparison](/odds) pages show prices side by side.

Say a SportyBet code has five selections with total odds of 12.60, and you want to place it on Bet9ja with ₦2,000. After conversion, four selections match and one, a corners market, is not offered. The four matched legs total 9.80 on Bet9ja. Your potential return falls from ₦25,200 to ₦19,600, but your chance of winning is also higher because there is one fewer leg to land. ## Tips for better conversions

- **Convert early.** The closer you get to kickoff, the more likely a selection is to drop out.
- **Stick to mainstream markets.** 1X2, double chance, over/under and BTTS convert most reliably.
- **Be wary of very large slips.** Each extra leg is another chance for a match or market to fail, and another chance for the bet to lose.
- **Never buy codes.** Converting a code does not make its picks any more likely to win, and anyone selling "guaranteed" codes is a red flag.

## FAQ

### Can every booking code be converted?

Most codes built on mainstream football markets convert well, but not every selection will. If the target bookmaker does not list the event, does not offer the same market or line, or the match has already started, that selection cannot be converted and will be flagged.

### Will the odds be the same after conversion?

Rarely. Each bookmaker sets its own prices, so the converted slip will usually have slightly different total odds. Sometimes they are higher, sometimes lower. Check the new total before staking, and compare prices across bookmakers if you want the best return.

### Why did my converted slip have fewer selections?

One or more selections could not be matched. The usual reasons are an event not covered by the target bookmaker, a market or line it does not offer, or a match that has kicked off. The converter shows which selections dropped and why.

### Does converting a code guarantee the same result?

No. Conversion copies the selections as closely as possible, but it does not change the chance of those picks winning. Settlement rules can also vary slightly between bookmakers, so read the market rules if you are unsure.
