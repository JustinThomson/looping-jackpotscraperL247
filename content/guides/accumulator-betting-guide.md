An accumulator is a single bet that combines two or more selections, where every selection must win for the bet to pay out. Because the odds of each leg are multiplied together, a small stake can return a large amount, which is why accas are so popular across Africa, Europe and, as parlays, in the United States. This accumulator betting guide explains how they work, how to calculate returns and why the risk grows with every leg you add.

## What is an accumulator bet?

An accumulator, usually shortened to **acca**, links several selections into one bet. You might pick four home wins in the weekend's football. If all four win, you are paid at the combined odds. If any one loses, the whole bet loses.

The same bet has different names around the world:

- **Accumulator or acca** in the UK, Ireland, Nigeria, Ghana and much of Africa
- **Multi or multibet** in South Africa, Kenya and other markets
- **Parlay** in the United States
- **Combo** at some European bookmakers

Specific types of multiple bets also have names based on the number of legs: a **double** has two selections, a **treble** three, and a **four-fold** four.

## How accumulator odds multiply

To find the total odds of an accumulator, multiply the decimal odds of every selection.

**Total odds = odds 1 × odds 2 × odds 3 × ...**

**Potential return = stake × total odds**

### Worked example

Suppose you build a four-fold with these selections:

| Selection | Decimal odds | Implied probability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Team A to win | 1.80 | 55.6% |
| Team B to win | 2.10 | 47.6% |
| Over 2.5 goals in Match C | 1.65 | 60.6% |
| Team D to win | 2.40 | 41.7% |

Total odds: 1.80 × 2.10 × 1.65 × 2.40 = **14.97**.

A ₦1,000 stake would return about **₦14,969** if all four win. At R100, the return would be about R1,497.

Now look at the chance of winning. Multiplying the implied probabilities gives 0.556 × 0.476 × 0.606 × 0.417 ≈ **6.7%**, roughly one in fifteen. Each selection on its own looks reasonable, but together they are unlikely to all land. Our [bet calculator](/tools/bet-calculator) works out returns for any combination.

## The hidden cost: margins compound

Every price carries a bookmaker margin. When you combine selections, those margins multiply too. If each of the four legs above had a 5% overround, the accumulator as a whole would carry roughly 1.05⁴ ≈ 1.216, or around 21.6%. That is why accas tend to favour the bookmaker more than singles, and why getting the best price on each leg makes such a difference. ## Acca bonuses and boosts

Many bookmakers in Africa and Europe offer accumulator bonuses to encourage multi-leg bets. Common types include:

- **Win boosts.** An extra percentage added to your winnings, rising with the number of legs. Some bookmakers offer large boosts on very long accas.
- **Acca insurance.** If one leg lets you down, you get your stake back, often as a free bet.
- **Price boosts.** Enhanced odds on popular combinations.

Terms vary a lot. Watch for a minimum number of selections, minimum odds per leg (often around 1.20 to 1.50), maximum bonus caps and restrictions on which markets count. Bonus amounts and rules change often, so check our [bonuses](/bonuses) page and the bookmaker's own terms before relying on one. A boost is useful only if the underlying odds are competitive.

## Same-game parlays in the United States

In the US, the **same-game parlay** (SGP) has become one of the most popular bet types. Rather than combining different games, you combine several outcomes from one game, such as a team to win, a player to score a touchdown and the total over a set number of points. Sportsbooks such as FanDuel and DraftKings helped make this format mainstream.

Because outcomes in the same game are related (if a quarterback throws for lots of yards, his receivers are more likely to hit their yardage too), sportsbooks cannot simply multiply the odds. They adjust the price for correlation, and same-game parlays typically carry higher margins than standard bets. Many bookmakers in Africa and Europe now offer a similar "bet builder" feature.

American parlay odds are calculated by converting each leg to decimal first. Two legs at −110 (1.91 each) give 1.91 × 1.91 ≈ 3.64, or about +264. A $10 parlay would return roughly $36.45.

## The risks of accumulators

Accas are entertaining, but the maths works against you:

- **Every leg must win.** One wrong result, often in the last match, wipes out the whole bet.
- **Probability falls fast.** Adding legs increases the payout but cuts the chance of winning more steeply than many people expect.
- **Margins stack up.** The more legs, the bigger the bookmaker's built-in edge.
- **Big wins are rare.** Stories of huge acca wins spread quickly on social media; the far more common losses do not.

Viral booking codes with 20 or 30 selections and odds in the thousands are, realistically, lottery tickets. If you copy them, use a small stake you are comfortable losing. ## Tips for smarter accas

- **Keep them short.** Doubles and trebles are far more likely to land than ten-folds.
- **Shop for prices on every leg.** Use our [odds comparison](/odds) pages to see which bookmaker offers the best total.
- **Consider system bets.** Bets such as a Trixie or Yankee cover combinations of selections so one loser does not sink everything, though they cost more to place.
- **Read the void rules.** If a match is postponed, most bookmakers treat that leg as void and recalculate the odds without it, but rules differ.
- **Use cash out with care.** Cashing out an acca early can lock in some profit, but the offer usually includes a margin. See [live betting and cash out](/guides/live-betting-and-cash-out).

## Bet responsibly

Accumulators are designed to be exciting, which makes it easy to bet more than you planned. Set a weekly budget, avoid chasing losses and take breaks. ## FAQ

### How do you calculate accumulator odds?

Multiply the decimal odds of every selection together. A treble at 1.50, 2.00 and 1.80 has total odds of 1.50 × 2.00 × 1.80 = 5.40, so a R50 stake returns R270 if all three win. Convert fractional or American odds to decimal first.

### What happens if one leg of my acca is postponed?

At most bookmakers, a postponed or abandoned match is treated as void, and that leg's odds become 1.00. The bet continues with the remaining selections at lower total odds. Rules on how long a match can be delayed vary, so check your bookmaker's terms.

### Is a parlay the same as an accumulator?

Yes. Parlay is the American name for an accumulator. A same-game parlay is a variation that combines several outcomes from one game, with odds adjusted because the outcomes are related.

### How many selections should an accumulator have?

There is no right number, but each extra leg reduces your chance of winning. Many bettors keep accas to between two and five selections. Larger accas pay more but lose far more often.

### Are acca bonuses worth it?

They can add value if you would place the bet anyway and the odds on each leg are competitive. Check the minimum legs, minimum odds per leg and the maximum bonus, and compare prices first, because a boost on poor odds may still be worse than a plain bet elsewhere.
