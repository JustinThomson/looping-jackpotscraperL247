Live betting, also called in-play betting, lets you place bets after an event has started, with odds that update constantly as the action unfolds. Cash out lets you settle a bet early for a guaranteed amount before the result is known, whether to lock in profit or cut a loss. This guide explains how live betting and cash out work, the main variations, and when cashing out tends to be poor value.

## What is live betting?

With a pre-match bet, the odds are fixed when you place it and the market closes at kickoff. With live betting, markets stay open during the match and prices change after every goal, card, injury or shift in momentum.

Popular live markets include:

- Match result and double chance
- Next goal or next team to score
- Over/under goals, points or corners
- Both teams to score
- Handicaps, which adjust as the score changes
- Set, game or point betting in tennis

Most bookmakers offer some form of live betting, often with match trackers or live streams. The range of in-play markets varies by country; in Germany, for example, regulation restricts in-play betting to a narrower set of markets.

## How live odds move

In-play prices are driven mainly by the score, the time remaining and events in the match.

### A worked example

Say you are watching a match where the home side started at 2.10 to win. Here is how their price might move:

| Moment | Score | Home win odds (illustrative) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kickoff | 0-0 | 2.10 |
| 30 minutes | 0-0 | 2.40 |
| 55 minutes, home team scores | 1-0 | 1.45 |
| 80 minutes | 1-0 | 1.15 |
| 85 minutes, red card for home side | 1-0 | 1.25 |

As time passes with the score level, draws become more likely and both teams' win prices drift. A goal swings prices sharply, and late in a game a one-goal lead becomes very valuable. These figures are illustrative; real prices depend on the teams and the bookmaker.

### Suspensions and bet delays

Bookmakers briefly **suspend markets** after key moments such as goals, penalties and VAR checks. Many also apply a short **bet delay** before accepting an in-play bet, so prices can change between tapping and confirmation. If the odds move, you may be asked to accept the new price. Settings such as "accept any odds changes" save time but mean you may get a worse price than you saw.

## What is cash out?

Cash out lets you settle a bet before the event finishes. The bookmaker offers you an amount based on the current odds of your bet winning. Accept it and the bet is settled immediately, regardless of what happens next.

### Full cash out

You take the entire offer and the bet is closed.

### Partial cash out

You cash out part of the bet and leave the rest running. For example, on a R200 bet you might cash out half for a guaranteed sum and let R100 ride.

### Auto cash out

You set a target value, and the bet cashes out automatically if the offer reaches it. This is useful when you cannot watch the match, but in fast-moving markets the offer can jump past your target, or a suspension can mean it never triggers.

### Edit bet

Some bookmakers let you change a bet after placing it, for example adding or removing selections from an accumulator or swapping a selection. Behind the scenes the bookmaker cashes out your original bet at its current value and uses that amount as the stake for the new bet. bet365 is one well-known example of a bookmaker offering this feature.

### Early payout offers

Some bookmakers settle certain bets as winners early if a condition is met, such as a team going two goals ahead. bet365's "2 Goals Ahead" early payout on football is a widely known example. These offers differ from cash out because the bookmaker pays the full winnings, not a reduced offer. Terms and eligible markets vary.

## How cash out values are calculated

A cash out offer is roughly your **potential return divided by the current odds of your bet winning**, minus a margin for the bookmaker.

Example: you placed R100 on a team at 3.00, so your potential return is R300. They are now winning and their live odds are 1.40. The "fair" cash out value would be about R300 ÷ 1.40 = **R214**. The bookmaker might offer something like R195 to R205, keeping the difference as margin.

This means cashing out almost always costs you a little compared with the fair value of your position. That is not necessarily a problem if you value certainty, but it adds up over time.

## Pros and cons of cash out

| Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- |
| Locks in profit when a bet is going well | Offers include a margin, so you usually get less than fair value |
| Can reduce losses when a bet is going badly | Encourages emotional, in-the-moment decisions |
| Gives flexibility if circumstances change (injury, red card) | Not always available; markets suspend at key moments |
| Partial cash out lets you balance risk and reward | Regular cashing out can lower your long-run returns |

## When cash out is poor value

Cashing out tends to work against you when:

- **You still believe in the bet.** If you thought 3.00 was a good price before kickoff and nothing important has changed, cashing out sells your position at a discount.
- **You cash out out of nerves.** Late in a match with your team leading, nervousness is natural, but the cash out offer already reflects the likely outcome, minus the bookmaker's cut.
- **You cash out losing bets automatically.** Taking a small amount back on a bet that is behind can feel sensible, but you are again selling at a discounted price.
- **You cash out accumulators habitually.** Acca cash out offers can carry bigger margins because the bookmaker prices every remaining leg.

Cash out can be reasonable when genuinely new information appears, such as an injury to a key player, or when locking in a sum matters more to you than the expected value. ## Tips for live betting

- **Set limits before the match.** In-play betting is fast and it is easy to place more bets than planned.
- **Beware of stream delays.** TV and streaming feeds can lag behind real time, so prices may already reflect events you have not seen.
- **Compare live prices.** In-play margins are often higher than pre-match. Check our [odds comparison](/odds) pages where live prices are available.
- **Do not chase.** Trying to win back a pre-match loss with live bets is a common route to bigger losses.
- **Use the tools.** Our [bet calculator](/tools/bet-calculator) helps you work out returns quickly, and our [accumulator betting guide](/guides/accumulator-betting-guide) explains how acca cash out offers are affected by multiple legs.

## Bet responsibly

Live betting is designed to be fast and exciting, which makes it one of the riskier ways to bet for anyone prone to impulsive decisions. Set deposit and time limits, take breaks and stop if it stops being fun. You must be 18 or over (21+ in most US states, 25 in Uganda). ## FAQ

### Is cash out a good idea?

It depends on your goals. Cash out offers usually include a margin, so over many bets it tends to reduce returns. It can make sense when significant new information appears or when you value a guaranteed amount more than the chance of a bigger payout.

### Why is cash out sometimes unavailable?

Bookmakers suspend cash out during key moments such as goals, penalties and VAR reviews, and some markets or bet types do not support it at all. Cash out may also be withdrawn on bets placed with bonus funds or free bets.

### What is the difference between full and partial cash out?

Full cash out settles your entire bet for the offered amount. Partial cash out lets you settle a portion and leave the rest of your stake running, so you lock in some return while keeping some exposure to the result.

### Why do live odds change so quickly?

Live odds update after every significant event and as time passes. Goals, red cards and injuries shift the likelihood of each outcome, and bookmakers adjust prices automatically, often within seconds.

### Does auto cash out always trigger at my target?

Not always. If the offer jumps past your target after a goal, or the market is suspended at the key moment, auto cash out may trigger at a different value or not at all. Check your bookmaker's rules.
