A value bet is one where the odds on offer are higher than the true chance of the outcome justifies. To find value bets, you need to compare prices across bookmakers, convert odds into implied probability, weigh that against your own estimate, and stake in a disciplined way. This guide explains how to find value bets in practical terms. It is not a route to guaranteed profit, because even well-judged bets lose often, but it is the foundation of thinking about betting more clearly.

## What is a value bet?

Every price implies a probability. Decimal odds of 2.50 imply 1 ÷ 2.50 = 40%. If you believe the outcome actually has a 45% chance, the price offers value: in the long run, bets like this would be expected to return more than they cost.

**Expected value (EV) = (your probability × decimal odds) − 1**

Example: you estimate a 45% chance and the odds are 2.50. EV = (0.45 × 2.50) − 1 = 0.125, or +12.5% per unit staked. A R100 bet has an expected value of +R12.50.

If the odds were 2.00 instead, EV = (0.45 × 2.00) − 1 = −0.10, or −10%. Same team, same match, but no longer a value bet.

Two things matter here: **the price** you get and **how accurate your estimate is**. You control the first far more than the second.

## How to find value bets: start with line shopping

Line shopping simply means checking several bookmakers and taking the best available price. It is the most reliable, low-effort way to improve your results, because you do not need to be better than the bookmaker at predicting matches; you just avoid paying more than necessary.

Consider one selection priced at three bookmakers:

| Bookmaker | Odds | Return on ₦5,000 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bookmaker A | 1.85 | ₦9,250 |
| Bookmaker B | 1.95 | ₦9,750 |
| Bookmaker C | 2.02 | ₦10,100 |

Taking 2.02 instead of 1.85 means ₦850 more on a winning bet. Over hundreds of bets, that gap can be the difference between losing steadily and something much closer to break-even or better. Across an accumulator, the difference compounds on every leg.

## Understand the margin

Bookmakers build a margin into their odds, so the implied probabilities in a market add up to more than 100%. Learning to strip this out gives you a better picture of the "fair" price. Our guide to [bookmaker margin](/guides/bookmaker-margin-explained) walks through the calculation, and the [margin calculator](/tools/margin-calculator) does it for you.

As a quick example, if a market is priced Home 2.10, Draw 3.40, Away 3.60, the overround is about 4.8%. Removing it proportionally suggests fair odds of roughly 2.20, 3.56 and 3.77. If another bookmaker is offering 2.25 on the home win, that price is above the estimated fair value of the first market, which is a sign it is worth a closer look.

## Building your own probability estimates

To find value beyond line shopping, you need a view on the true probability. Ways bettors approach this include:

- **Using sharp market prices.** Some bookmakers and betting exchanges are known for efficient prices. Bettors often treat their no-margin price as a benchmark and look for softer bookmakers offering more.
- **Statistical models.** Expected goals (xG), team ratings and historical data can help produce probability estimates, though building a reliable model takes time and testing.
- **Specialist knowledge.** Following a lower league, women's football or a niche sport closely can help you spot information that prices have not fully reflected, though markets often adjust quickly.

Be honest about uncertainty. If your estimate is 52% and the price implies 50%, the edge is small and well within your margin of error. Value is only as good as the estimate behind it.

## Closing line value: a useful check

The **closing line** is the final price before an event starts, when the most information and money have shaped the market. **Closing line value (CLV)** measures whether you beat that price.

Example: you take 2.20 on a team and the odds close at 2.00. You beat the closing line by 2.20 ÷ 2.00 − 1 = **10%**.

Consistently beating the closing line is widely seen as a sign that your process is finding genuine value, even during spells when results go against you. It is not a guarantee of profit, but it is a more stable measure over a few hundred bets than wins and losses alone.

## Bankroll management and staking

Even with good prices, losing runs are inevitable. Sensible staking keeps you in control.

### Set a bankroll

Decide on a sum of money set aside specifically for betting, which you could lose without affecting your bills, savings or family. Never top it up from essential funds.

### Choose a staking plan

- **Flat staking.** Bet the same amount every time, for example 1% to 2% of your bankroll. Simple and robust.
- **Percentage staking.** Bet a fixed percentage of your current bankroll, so stakes shrink after losses and grow after wins.
- **Kelly criterion.** A formula that sizes bets by your estimated edge: stake fraction = (b × p − q) ÷ b, where b is decimal odds minus 1, p is your probability and q is 1 − p. At odds of 2.20 and a 50% estimate, b = 1.20, so (1.20 × 0.50 − 0.50) ÷ 1.20 ≈ 8.3% of bankroll. Because estimates are uncertain, full Kelly is aggressive; many bettors use a fraction of it or avoid it entirely.

With a R5,000 bankroll and 2% flat stakes, each bet is R100. A run of ten losses, which happens more often than people expect, costs R1,000 and leaves you with plenty to continue.

### Keep records

Track every bet: date, market, odds taken, closing odds, stake and result. Records show whether you are really finding value and help you avoid relying on memory, which tends to remember wins and forget losses. Our [bet calculator](/tools/bet-calculator) helps check returns.

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing likely with valuable.** A 1.20 favourite may win often but still be poor value.
- **Chasing losses.** Raising stakes to recover losses breaks any staking plan.
- **Overrating small samples.** Ten winning bets prove very little.
- **Ignoring bonus terms.** Promotions can add value but only on fair terms. See [betting bonuses explained](/guides/betting-bonuses-explained).
- **Expecting guarantees.** No method, tipster or system guarantees profit.

## Bet responsibly

Value betting is about making better decisions, not about turning betting into a reliable income. Most bettors lose over time. Set limits, treat betting as entertainment and stop if it causes stress. You must be 18 or over (21+ in most US states, 25 in Uganda). Visit our [responsible gambling](/responsible-gambling) page for help.

## FAQ

### What is a value bet in simple terms?

A value bet is one where the odds are higher than they should be given the real chance of the outcome. If you think a team has a 50% chance and the odds are 2.20, which implies about 45%, that bet offers value according to your estimate.

### Does value betting guarantee profit?

No. Value betting improves your expected returns if your estimates are accurate, but individual bets still lose often and losing runs can be long. Many bettors who look for value still lose overall, so only bet what you can afford to lose.

### What is line shopping?

Line shopping means comparing the odds for the same bet across several bookmakers and placing it wherever the price is best. It is one of the simplest ways to improve long-term returns because it reduces how much margin you pay.

### What is closing line value?

Closing line value measures how the odds you took compare with the final odds before the event started. If you consistently get better prices than the closing line, it suggests your bets are finding value, even if short-term results vary.

### How much should I stake per bet?

Many bettors use flat stakes of around 1% to 2% of a dedicated betting bankroll. This keeps individual losses small and makes losing runs manageable. Never stake money you need for essentials, and never increase stakes to chase losses.
