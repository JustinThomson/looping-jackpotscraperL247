Betting bonuses are incentives bookmakers offer to win new customers or keep existing ones, such as deposit matches, free bets and acca boosts. Their real value depends less on the headline amount and more on the terms: wagering requirements, minimum odds, expiry dates and whether your stake is returned. This guide has betting bonuses explained in plain terms, with worked examples so you can tell a genuinely useful offer from a flashy one.

## The main types of betting bonus

### Welcome bonus

A welcome bonus, also called a sign-up offer, is only available to new customers, usually on their first deposit or first bet. It can take any of the forms below. You can normally claim only one welcome bonus per person, household or payment method at each bookmaker.

### Deposit match

The bookmaker matches a percentage of your first deposit with bonus funds, for example 100% up to a set limit. Deposit R500 and you receive R500 in bonus funds. These funds usually come with wagering requirements before they can be withdrawn.

### Free bet

You receive a token to place a bet of a fixed size. The key detail is that most free bets are **stake not returned** (SNR). If you win, you get the winnings but not the free bet amount itself.

Example: a ₦5,000 free bet at odds of 3.00 wins ₦10,000 (₦15,000 minus the ₦5,000 stake), not ₦15,000.

### Bet and get

Place a qualifying bet with your own money, for example £10 at minimum odds of 1.50, and receive free bets in return, often whether your qualifying bet wins or loses. These offers are common in the UK and increasingly in the US.

### Second-chance bet or bet insurance

If your first bet loses, the bookmaker refunds your stake, usually as a free bet or bonus funds rather than cash. US sportsbooks often market this as a "first bet safety net" or "bet reset". It reduces the sting of a losing first bet but is worth less than a cash refund.

### Acca boosts and price boosts

Ongoing promotions for existing customers, such as an extra percentage on winning accumulators or enhanced odds on a featured market. Our [accumulator betting guide](/guides/accumulator-betting-guide) covers acca boosts in detail.

### No-deposit bonus

A small free bet or bonus credited just for registering or verifying your account. These are less common and usually carry strict terms and low withdrawal caps.

## Wagering requirements explained

A wagering requirement (also called a rollover or playthrough) is the amount you must bet before bonus funds or winnings from them can be withdrawn. It is usually expressed as a multiple, for example 10x the bonus or 5x the deposit plus bonus.

The basis matters a lot:

- **10x bonus** on a R1,000 bonus means R10,000 of bets.
- **10x deposit plus bonus** on a R1,000 deposit and R1,000 bonus means R20,000 of bets.

Other conditions usually apply:

- **Minimum odds.** Each qualifying bet may need odds of at least 1.50, 1.75 or higher. Accas may need minimum odds per leg.
- **Expiry.** You may have 7, 14 or 30 days to complete the rollover. Unused bonus funds disappear.
- **Excluded markets.** Some bookmakers exclude certain markets, or bets settled through cash out.
- **Maximum stake and maximum win.** Some bonuses limit the size of bets or the amount you can withdraw.

### Worked example: what is a bonus really worth?

Imagine a 100% deposit match up to R1,000 with a 10x bonus wagering requirement at minimum odds of 1.50. You deposit R1,000 and receive R1,000 in bonus funds.

- Turnover required: 10 × R1,000 = **R10,000**
- If the bookmaker's average margin on your bets is around 5%, your expected cost of that turnover is roughly 5% × R10,000 = **R500**
- Theoretical value of the bonus: R1,000 − R500 = **about R500**

Now change one term: 20x deposit plus bonus.

- Turnover required: 20 × R2,000 = **R40,000**
- Expected cost at 5%: **R2,000**
- That is more than the R1,000 bonus, so the offer has **negative theoretical value**.

These are long-run averages. Real results vary from bet to bet, and nothing guarantees you will clear a rollover or come out ahead. The point is that the wagering terms, not the headline number, decide whether a bonus is worth having. Our [margin calculator](/tools/margin-calculator) helps you check the margins on the markets you plan to use, and [bookmaker margin explained](/guides/bookmaker-margin-explained) shows how the maths works.

### Valuing a free bet

Because the stake is not returned, a free bet is worth less than its face value. For a R100 SNR free bet at odds of 3.00, you win R200 if it lands. If the true chance of winning is a little under one in three once the margin is taken into account, the expected value is around R60 to R65. Free bets tend to give the most value at medium to longer odds, because the lost stake is a smaller share of the payout.

## Regional rules that affect bonuses

Bonus rules depend heavily on where you live:

- **United Kingdom.** The Gambling Commission capped wagering requirements at 10x from January 2026 and introduced other restrictions on how promotions are structured. See our [UK betting sites](/betting-sites/united-kingdom) page.
- **Sweden.** Licensed operators may only offer a bonus on the first occasion a customer plays with them, so ongoing reload offers are not allowed.
- **Other European markets.** Countries such as Spain, Italy, Germany and the Netherlands restrict bonus advertising or the way bonuses are offered to varying degrees. Our [guide to betting in Europe](/guides/betting-in-europe-guide) summarises the regulators.
- **United States.** Offers vary by state, and some states restrict how promotions can be advertised or described. Check our [US betting sites](/betting-sites/united-states) page.
- **Africa.** Bonuses are common in South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya and Ghana, with terms that change frequently. See the country pages, such as South Africa and Nigeria.

## How to compare betting bonuses

Use this checklist when comparing offers:

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| What is the wagering multiple and basis? | 10x bonus is far easier than 20x deposit plus bonus |
| What are the minimum odds? | Higher minimums mean riskier qualifying bets |
| How long do I have? | Short expiry makes rollover harder |
| Is the free bet stake returned? | SNR free bets are worth less than face value |
| Is there a maximum win or withdrawal? | Caps can limit real value |
| Which payment methods qualify? | Some exclude certain e-wallets or vouchers |
| Are the odds competitive? | A bonus at a bookmaker with poor odds can cost more in the long run |

Our [bonuses](/bonuses) page lists current offers with their key terms, and our bookmaker reviews explain how each operator's promotions work. Bonus amounts change often, so always confirm the latest terms on the bookmaker's site before you deposit.

## Bet responsibly

Bonuses are marketing. They are designed to get you betting, and it is easy to bet more than planned while chasing a rollover. Only claim offers you understand, never deposit more than you can afford just to unlock a bigger bonus, and set limits from the start. You must be 18 or over (21+ in most US states, 25 in Uganda). ## FAQ

### What does 10x wagering mean?

It means you must bet ten times a stated amount before you can withdraw bonus funds or winnings from them. For a R500 bonus with 10x bonus wagering, you need to place R5,000 of qualifying bets. Check whether the multiple applies to the bonus only or the deposit plus bonus.

### Can I withdraw a betting bonus immediately?

Usually not. Bonus funds and free bets normally have to be wagered or used first, and only the resulting winnings can be withdrawn once the terms are met. Your own deposited cash can usually be withdrawn, but doing so may cancel an active bonus.

### Are free bets really free?

You do not risk your own money on the free bet itself, but there is usually a qualifying deposit or bet first, and the stake is generally not returned when you win. That makes a free bet worth noticeably less than its face value.

### Which betting bonus is best?

The best bonus is the one with fair terms that suits how you already bet: low wagering, reasonable minimum odds, a realistic time limit and a bookmaker with competitive odds. A smaller offer with simple terms is often worth more than a large one with heavy rollover.

### Can I claim more than one welcome bonus?

You can usually claim one welcome bonus per bookmaker, but you may be able to claim offers at different bookmakers. In some countries, such as Sweden, regulations limit bonuses further. Opening multiple accounts at the same bookmaker to claim repeat bonuses breaks the terms and can lead to account closure.
